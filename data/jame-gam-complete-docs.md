# 2. What Jame Gam is

Jame Gam is a Hack Club game-development program built around participating in designated game jams. During a selected jam, participants make a game within the jam's timeframe/theme, submit it to the external jam, then submit it to Jame Gam for review.

The reward model is **not winner-takes-all**. Jame Gam's stated goal is to reward participants who put in meaningful effort and learn, even if their game is not highly polished or does not place well in the external jam.

The external jam's voting/ranking does not determine whether you qualify for the normal Jame Gam effort-based prize.

### Beginner friendliness

You do **not** have to already be an expert game developer. Many participants use Jame Gam as a first game jam or first serious game project.

The important part is that the submission is a real playable game and there is enough evidence to show meaningful work.

# 3. Participation lifecycle

A typical Jame Gam cycle works like this:

1. Jame Gam selects/designates an external game jam.
2. Read that jam's itch.io rules before beginning.
3. Join the jam on itch.io if required by that jam.
4. Build the game during the allowed jam period.
5. Submit the game to the external jam.
6. Near/after the end, Jame Gam publishes its own submission form.
7. Submit the project to Jame Gam with the requested evidence/repository.
8. Wait for manual review.
9. If accepted, prize selection becomes available.
10. Select the prize before the shop window closes.
11. Fulfillment is processed separately.

Do **not** assume an old Jame Gam submission form remains valid for the next jam.

# 6. Solo vs. teams

You may work solo or on a team.

For the current Cozy Fall discussion, helper Jack Jacobson answered that there was **no team-size limit**. Because external jams can have their own rules, verify this against the selected jam before treating it as universal forever.

### Team members and prizes

The important rule is **individual contribution / significant effort**.

A team member can qualify even if their main role is not programming. Examples include:

- art
- music/audio
- level design
- writing
- game design
- programming
- other meaningful production work

Ruben clarified in a team question that the only core requirement was **significant effort**. Git commits help reviewers but are not a strict requirement.

A participant should keep evidence of their own contribution, especially for non-code roles.

Good evidence can include:

- source-control history
- committed art/audio/assets
- screenshots
- work-in-progress files
- screen recordings
- local files/history
- Lapse or another development recording
- Hackatime for coding
- other credible development evidence

### Teammates who are not in Hack Club

The FAQ says you **may** work with somebody who is not in Hack Club, but that teammate does not receive a Jame Gam prize unless they join Hack Club and otherwise qualify.

The game still needs to meet Jame Gam's submission/open-source requirements.

# 9. Open-source requirement

The official FAQ says the game is required to be **open source**.

If the project uses paid/licensed marketplace content that cannot legally be redistributed in the repository:

- do not illegally publish the licensed asset
- link/reference the dependency instead

The project's own code/work should still be reviewable.

### Git / commit history

Git history is strongly recommended because it helps:

- prevent lost work
- show the development process
- make review easier
- establish who contributed what

But helpers have clarified that a perfect commit history is **not a strict requirement**.

A solo project uploaded late to Git is not automatically disqualified.

---

# 10. Significant effort — the central review rule

There is **no fixed minimum-hour requirement** in the official FAQ.

The purpose is to reward genuine effort and learning, not to impose a rigid hours threshold.

Reviewers can use evidence such as:

- Hackatime
- GitHub/source code
- commit history
- amount/quality of project content
- screenshots
- local development files
- videos/screen recordings
- art/audio work
- other credible proof

### What can cause rejection?

Helper guidance gives examples such as:

- the game outright does not work and the participant/reviewer cannot get it working
- the participant contributed extremely little
- a project appears essentially 100% AI-generated with little/no development evidence
- suspicious evidence such as one final "Add files via upload" commit plus an obviously generated project can trigger concern

This is not a checklist where one imperfect Git history automatically means rejection. Reviewers look at the whole submission.

# 11. Hackatime

Hackatime is **optional**.

The FAQ says it can help demonstrate effort and may sometimes lead to an extra reward such as stickers.

Do not tell participants they need a particular number of Hackatime hours unless a newer jam-specific rule explicitly creates one.

For teammates doing art/music/design, use other evidence too; Hackatime is not a universal representation of contribution.

# 12. AI policy

The official FAQ currently says:

- **AI may not be used for art**
- **AI may not be used for music**
- AI is permitted for **coding assistance**, but it must assist the participant rather than replace them. The participant must still make and understand the substantial body of the work; fully or nearly fully AI-generated projects with little genuine participant contribution are not acceptable.
- an individual external game jam may have stricter/different rules, so its itch.io rules must also be checked

Organizer Augie also asked participants not to write AI-generated messages in the Jame Gam channel.

### Bot-answer rule

Do not generalize the coding-assistance allowance into "AI is allowed for everything." It is not.

If the selected external jam bans or restricts AI more strongly, the external jam rule applies too.

---

# 13. Double-dipping / submitting the same work elsewhere

The FAQ says you cannot receive rewards twice for the **same work** through another event such as Stardance.

You may continue working on the project after the Jame Gam submission and submit the **new continued work** elsewhere.

The distinction is:

- same already-rewarded work → not eligible to be rewarded again
- meaningful new work after the first submission → may be eligible elsewhere, subject to that program's rules

When uncertain, escalate rather than interpreting edge cases aggressively.

# 14. Submission process

## External jam submission

During the game jam, submit to the external jam's itch.io page according to its rules.

## Jame Gam submission

Near the end, Jame Gam posts a separate form for Jame Gam review.

The generic FAQ says participants normally have **4 days after the jam ends** to submit to Jame Gam.

However, **jam-specific organizer announcements can override that generic window**.

Example: for the August/Brackeys cycle on 2026-08-30, Augie posted a Jame Gam form and explicitly said it would close in **24 hours**.

Therefore:

> Never answer a deadline question using the generic "4 days" rule without checking the latest jam-specific announcement.

### If somebody missed itch.io submission

During the Brackeys cycle, Augie's Jame Gam submission announcement explicitly said that people who missed the itch submission could still use the Jame Gam form.

That was a **specific exception for that cycle**. Do not promise the same exception for every jam.

### Late Jame Gam submission

Late submissions may sometimes be handled manually. In one September support case, Augie asked the participant to send their game and then supplied a password so they could submit.

This proves manual exceptions can exist, not that late submission is always guaranteed.

Correct answer:

> Ask in `#jame-gam-help`; an organizer may be able to help, but don't assume an extension is automatic.

Do not invent:

- queue positions
- "you are #37"
- exact remaining-submission counts
- guaranteed ordering

### Review ETA

Review speed varies by jam and workload.

In early September:
- Jack said to expect a review within roughly a week

These are historical operational estimates, **not a permanent SLA**.

A safe answer is:

> Reviews are manual and not strictly ordered. Current ETA depends on the batch; check the newest organizer/helper update in `#jame-gam-help`.

### Re-review / missed reviewer message

If a reviewer reached out and the participant missed the message, ask in `#jame-gam-help` and explain what happened. Re-review can be handled manually; do not promise it automatically.

# 22. Physical fulfillment

Physical prizes are not necessarily shipped from Hack Club HQ.

Ruben explained that Jame Gam usually tries to source physical prizes **locally or wherever they can obtain them most economically**.

This helps with availability and shipping, but local fulfillment is not guaranteed for every item/country.

### Important: customs

This topic had conflicting intermediate answers, and the **newest organizer clarification wins**.

The current safe policy from Augie (2026-09-12):

> Hack Club/Jame Gam **does not generally cover customs**.

A vendor may sometimes include/import duties in its checkout price; that is different from Jame Gam promising to reimburse customs later.

Do **not** tell someone:

> "You definitely won't have to pay customs."

That was said earlier by a helper and was later corrected by the organizer.

### Local fulfillment

Helpers try to fulfill locally **when possible**. It is reasonable to ask support about a local vendor, but do not guarantee they will use a participant-supplied store/link.

### Stickers and customs

Augie said Jame Gam stickers come from Hack Club HQ. This can create customs in some destinations.

The prize-selection flow includes a checkbox equivalent to:

> don't ship me stickers

for people who would rather avoid that shipment.

# 34. FAQ — ready-to-use bot answers

## "How do I do Jame Gam?"

Join the designated game jam, make a game during its allowed timeframe, submit it to the jam, then fill out the Jame Gam submission form when the team posts it. Jame Gam reviews the work for significant effort; accepted participants can choose a prize.

## "Do I have to win?"

No. Normal Jame Gam prizes are based on meaningful effort/learning, not placing in the external jam.

## "How many hours do I need?"

There is no fixed minimum-hour requirement in the current FAQ. Reviewers look for significant effort and evidence of development.

## "Is Hackatime required?"

No. It is optional and can help prove coding effort; it may sometimes qualify you for an extra item such as stickers.

## "Can I use AI?"

AI may assist with debugging, explaining errors or code, API questions, small snippets, and limited coding suggestions while the participant remains the actual developer. It must not generate essentially the whole game, write nearly all of the code, or produce work the participant does not meaningfully understand. There is no reliable percentage limit; treat AI as an assistant, not the developer. AI art and AI music, including sprites and textures, are not allowed. Also obey the selected external jam's own AI rules.

## "Do I need GitHub?"

The game needs to be open source/reviewable. Git and meaningful commits are strongly recommended, but helpers have said a perfect commit history itself is not a strict requirement.

## "Can I use Unity/Godot/GameMaker/etc.?"

Yes, generally. Any technology is okay if it produces a working playable game and follows the current jam rules. The FAQ explicitly excludes Scratch.

## "Can I work with a team?"

Yes.

## "Does an artist/music person get a prize too?"

They can, if they are an eligible participant and show significant individual effort. Keep proof of the work.

## "Can a non-Hack-Club friend be on my team?"

Yes, but the FAQ says they do not get a Jame Gam prize unless they join Hack Club and qualify.

## "Is there a queue number for reviews?"

No public queue number is currently provided, and reviews are not necessarily processed in exact submission order.

## "How long will review take?"

It varies. Historical estimates around early September were roughly a week/end-of-next-week, but that is not a guaranteed SLA. Check current support updates.

## "Where will I get the prize-selection message?"

On Slack from the Jame Gam bot.

## "When does my prize shop close?"

The FAQ currently says 4 days after it opens for you following approval.

## "Will Hack Club pay customs?"

Do not promise that. The latest organizer clarification says customs are **not generally covered**. Local sourcing is attempted when possible.

## "Can you fulfill locally?"

Often the team tries to buy locally or use the most practical vendor, but it depends on the item/country. Ask support with the exact item and location.

## "Can I change the prize model/color?"

Maybe, case-by-case. Ask the fulfillment team before assuming the substitution is allowed.

## "I missed the deadline. Am I cooked?"

Ask `#jame-gam-help`. Manual exceptions have been made, but they are not guaranteed.

## "The site says my submission disappeared."

This has happened as a dashboard bug before. If you have confirmation, don't repeatedly resubmit blindly; ask support and provide the confirmation/context.

## "Why was I rejected?"

Common causes include a non-working submission that cannot be resolved, extremely little contribution, or little credible evidence of genuine development. Ask the reviewer for the exact reason.

# 40. Final operational summary

If you only remember the important rules:

- Make a real playable game during the selected jam.
- Read the external jam rules.
- Jame Gam rewards **significant effort**, not only winners.
- No fixed minimum hours.
- Hackatime is optional.
- Keep proof of your work.
- The game must be open source/reviewable.
- Scratch is excluded.
- AI art/music are prohibited by the Jame Gam FAQ; coding assistance is allowed but discouraged.
- Teams are allowed; non-code contributors can qualify.
- The same work cannot be rewarded twice.
- Submit to the external jam **and** the later Jame Gam form.
- Jam-specific deadline announcements override generic old timings.
- Reviews are manual, not strictly ordered, and have no public queue number.
- Prize selection arrives on Slack from the Jame Gam bot.
- Accepted users currently choose one normal prize or two listed indie games.
- Shop currently closes four days after opening for an approved submission.
- Physical fulfillment is often sourced locally/efficiently where possible.
- **Customs are not generally covered** — newer organizer clarification supersedes earlier helper claims.
- For account/order/submission edge cases, use `#jame-gam-help`.
- Never answer a current-jam question from an old jam's rules without checking freshness.
