  Why it got through

  That thread started with a real question ("anyone know why my sprite in
  aseprite wont load?") which pixie answered. So thread was live for pixie.
  Then:

  1. PROBLEM_WORD listed bare contractions — isnt|isn't|wont|cant|doesnt. "ridit
  isn't" matched on isn't.
  2. Match cleared couldNeedHelp, paid for classifier call, classifier said
  HELP_NEEDED.
  3. Same match cleared looksLikeHelpRequest, so HELP_ONLY let it speak.

  One bad regex entry defeated both gates. Bare isn't is ordinary negation, not
  a problem report.

  Fix — two layers

  Gate: contraction now only counts when attached to a failing verb.

  // before: isn't matched anywhere
  const BROKEN_VERB =
    /\b(?:wont|won't|cant|can't|doesnt|doesn't|isnt|isn't|didnt|didn't|not)\s+(?
  :\w+\s+){0,2}(?:work|load|run|build|render|show|connect|compile|export|launch|
  install|sync|...)\b/i;

  "sprite wont load" still passes. "ridit isn't" doesn't. Plus 3-word floor on
  looksLikeHelpRequest — fragments aren't requests, code exempt.

  Output: unaddressed, pixie may not hand work back. Short reply ending in ?
  gets dropped instead of posted.

  Verified against real messages from that thread:

  silent   "ridit isn't"
  silent   "yo ridit I got a song for y"
  REPLIED  "anyone know why my sprite in aseprite wont load?"

  One limit, honest

  Output guard only catches replies that are nothing but a question. Live probe
  on vague-but-real "the thing isnt showing" got "what thing? could be a bunch

  question, then gives content, so it posts. Didn't widen it: killing those

  fragment class, guard handles the pure-clarify class.

  "is this really needed? how fast would it make"

  Honest: not as much as you'd hope. Let me not oversell it.

  Your 4763ms p50 breaks down roughly as network → prefill (reading the
  ~7.5k-token corpus) → generation (writing the answer). Retrieval only shrinks
  the middle one. Realistic saving is ~300ms–1.5s off p50 — not a halving.
  Generation dominates, and your p99 of 21.9s is the model stalling, which
  retrieval does nothing for.

  Where it does pay off:
  - Accuracy. 8k tokens of context for a question that needs one paragraph is
  why answer_docs sits at 23%. A focused prompt grounds better. This is really
  an accuracy change with a speed side-effect.
  - The cache. cache_hit is 3 of 114 — 2.6% — because it matches the exact
  question string. "how do i export" and "how do you export sprites" are two
  misses today. Matching by meaning could push that to 20-30%, and a cache hit
  is ~0ms instead of 4763ms. That's the actual speed win, not the retrieval.

  So: worth doing, wrong reason. I'll scope it as measure-first — chunk the
  corpus and prove the number before adding any embedding dependency.
